02 · The why
Why we’re making this change
The goal is to save everyone money, you and the plan as a whole. Priority Health’s renewal of today’s two plans came in 16.76% higher, so we looked for a better way.
1
Lower costs for everyone

The new base plan’s total premium is about 13% below renewing today’s plans as is. You pay 8% of that premium, so a lower premium means a lower share for you than the renewal would have brought. On average, your share stays close to 2026.

2
HSA money for everyone on the plan

$1,750 single / $2,750 family a year from the company, for everyone on the medical plan. Today only people on the HSA plan get it.

3
An embedded deductible

Each person meets their own $3,500, and a family tops out at $7,000. If one family member needs care, the company’s $2,750 family HSA money covers all but $750 of that person’s deductible.

4
One plan design, two networks

Both options have the same deductible, coinsurance, out-of-pocket max, and prescription copays. The only difference is the network.

What this is not: a way for Perceptive to spend less on you. Perceptive keeps paying 92% of the base plan premium and adds HSA money for everyone on the medical plan. Its total spending on medical and HSA money goes up for 2027.

05 · Choosing
HMO or POS: which one fits you?
Start with where you and your family get care.
Most people
The HSA HMO likely fits if…
  • You and your family get your care in Michigan
  • Your doctors are in the Priority Health network
  • You want the lower paycheck cost
  • You want peace of mind for emergencies: both plans cover emergency care anywhere
Consider the buy-up
The HSA POS may fit if…
  • You or someone you cover lives outside Michigan
  • You travel often and want routine care covered outside Michigan
  • You get regular care from a provider outside Michigan
  • You want out-of-network coverage, at a higher cost
Either way: same deductible, same company HSA money, same drug list, and no referrals needed to see a specialist. Not sure if your doctor is in network? Search at priorityhealth.com, or ask us. We’ll check with you.

How the HSA works
A health savings account is your money, for health costs now and later. Both 2027 plans are HSA plans.
1
Company money in
$1,750 single
$2,750 family

A year, deposited into your HSA each payday.

2

Up to $4,500 / $9,000

Total for 2027, including the company’s money. Plus $1,000 if you’re 55 or older.

➜
3
Tax-free three ways
In, growth, and out

Pre-tax going in, tax-free growth, and tax-free when used for qualified health costs.

➜
4
Yours to keep
It rolls over

Unused money stays in your account every year, and goes with you if you change jobs.

Use it now

Deductible and coinsurance, prescriptions, dental and vision care, and more.

Use it later

At 65 or older, it can pay Medicare premiums (not Medigap), tax-free.

Good to know

You can’t add to an HSA once you’re on Medicare, or if you also have other non-HSA coverage or a general-purpose FSA (including a spouse’s).

08 · If you’re on the traditional plan today
What to do if you’re on the G20 POS
The traditional plan ends December 31, 2026. A few steps make January 1 smooth.
1
Pick a 2027 plan by November 20

Choose the HSA HMO or the HSA POS buy-up in Employee Navigator. Plans don’t roll over this year. If you don’t enroll, you won’t have medical coverage on January 1.

2
Know how costs work on an HSA plan

Instead of copays up front, you pay toward the deductible first for most care (preventive care is free). The company’s HSA money helps cover it.

3
Have a prior authorization? Ask your provider to resubmit

Approvals don’t carry over automatically between plans. Your provider sends a new request for your 2027 plan that references your current approval. Doing it in December means it’s in place on day one.

4
Prescriptions: same drug list

Priority Health’s drug list is the same on the HSA plans. What changes is timing: most prescriptions count toward the deductible first.

On the S23 HSA plan today? That plan ends too, so steps 1 and 3 apply to you as well.
